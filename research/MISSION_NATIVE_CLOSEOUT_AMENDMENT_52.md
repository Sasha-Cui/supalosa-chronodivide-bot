## Resignation diagnosis and literal endpoint contract

The pinned Supalosa implementation attempts `quitGame` when it has no army,
production building, or deployable MCV. It may still own non-production
buildings at that point, so this condition is not the project's literal victory
endpoint. The trace harness already replaces `quitGame` with a no-op counter for
both participants. Consequently an attempt is observable but cannot terminate
the game; the engine finish predicate remains the only technical censoring
boundary.

The next technical version will preserve that symmetric interception and will:

1. record candidate and baseline attempt counts separately;
2. prove by deterministic test that the installed replacement cannot invoke the
   original `quitGame` action;
3. permit recorded attempts because they are suppressed rather than outcomes;
4. forbid using an attempt as a win, loss, draw, score, or stopping rule; and
5. continue to prohibit serialization or inspection of winner, score, terminal
   building counts, or defeat cause.

This change prevents Supalosa's heuristic surrender from substituting for
physical destruction of all enemy buildings. It applies symmetrically to both
methods and does not alter either policy's in-game commands.

## Ownership-loss contract evidence

A naturally occurring ownership-loss transition is a rare exposure event, not
the study endpoint. Requiring one in each small live seed panel encourages
repeated diagnostic panels until a rare trace appears. The prospective gate
therefore replaces that stochastic occurrence requirement with a conjunction
of independently sealed checks:

1. exact policy validation proves V37 differs from V36 only by
   `recoverAfterPredecessorOwnershipLoss: true`;
2. the external adapter test proves the V37 option reaches the actual mission
   factory;
3. the core state-transition test proves that after the 120-tick grace period a
   historical owner with zero current owners triggers same-update recovery,
   while a current owner prevents recovery;
4. the telemetry validator proves that ownership observation followed by an
   empty current-owner set and bounded recovery is accepted, and that the old
   historical-owner latch is rejected;
5. fresh live all-country reciprocal traces must contain at least one immediate
   ownership observation and at least one no-owner recovery, proving both live
   controller interfaces; and
6. every live fallback must be owned, recovered, or legitimately truncated at
   its outcome-free trace boundary.

These checks validate the repair contract without claiming that the rare
transition occurred naturally or affected competitive performance.
