\section{Deterministic Tactical Evidence}
\label{sec:frames}

Screenshots are selected by a protocol fixed before replay. Within each
registered category, the case is the hash-minimum eligible record and frame
updates are fixed from public state transitions. A passive renderer reads only
the public game API\@. Independent renderer-off and renderer-on runs have
identical trajectories, endpoints, action calls, and production calls. All
\ReplayCount{} replays and \FrameCount{} frames passed immutable hash checks.
The images illustrate observed tactics; quantitative claims continue to rest
on the complete replications.

Figure~\ref{fig:hfo-closeout} illustrates the decision boundary raised by
literal adjudication. When combatants block access, removing them can be
instrumental. Once the remaining structure is exposed, continuing to hunt all
units would delay an already available win. The image sequence records the
state transition; the replicated retarget studies provide the policy-level
evidence.

\subsection{An honest liveness failure}

The bottom row of Figure~\ref{fig:hfo-failure} is qualitatively different from
a winning advantage that merely needs faster targeting: the candidate has lost
its mobile force. The game is correctly scored as a draw and is preserved
rather than replaced by a more attractive replay. The deployed policy is
reliable, not infallible; its remaining failures include liveness and
force-preservation errors.

\subsection{Design implications}

The positive mechanisms share a structure: they change a small conditional
subprogram rather than replace the complete agent. West rush/guard changes
opening force allocation, bottom retarget changes liveness after a certified
stall, and Peak changes one spatial-scope predicate. Exact inactive-cell traces
matter here. Without them, a higher aggregate win rate could be caused by an
unintended change to economy, timing, or a previously strong start. This
suggests a useful design pattern for scripted RTS agents: treat map profiles as
testable conditional programs with explicit activation domains.

Literal victory also makes objective completion a separate control problem.
The middle row of Figure~\ref{fig:hfo-closeout} shows why
\emph{attack continuously} is insufficient as a specification. A policy must
decide whether combatants currently block access, whether enough force can be
spared from home defense, and whether destroying a reachable structure ends
the game sooner than clearing the army. Progress-gated retargeting supplies
memory--the building count and aggregate hit points must actually change--while
the force and threat gates prevent a blind late-game charge. The remaining
tick-cap case shows the missing complement: if mobile force is gone, retarget
frequency cannot recover the game.

Finally, opponent code is part of the evaluation distribution. The Advanced
reversal is too large to describe as ordinary seed noise and survived paired
country/start/slot control. A technically accurate opponent-style detector does
not solve that problem unless each routed expert is itself competent. Future
work should therefore train over multiple independent opponents or optimize a
worst-opponent objective before adding adaptive routing. The present evidence
supports reliable Supalosa-specific strength and exposes, rather than resolves,
the broader robustness problem.
