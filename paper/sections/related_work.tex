\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Configuring scripted RTS agents.}
Evolutionary optimization of hand-authored game-bot parameters is established
practice. Fern{\'a}ndez-Ares et al. optimized rule-system constants for Planet
Wars~\cite{fernandezAres2011optimizing}; Young and Hawes evolved StarCraft goal
priorities~\cite{young2012goal}; Othman et al. optimized XML-configured tactical
behavior~\cite{othman2012starcraft}; and Liu et al. evolved microbehavior
parameters and examined transfer to unseen combat scenarios
\cite{liu2016microbehaviors}. Mora et al. showed specifically that noisy RTS
fitness requires repeated combats, multiple maps, and incumbent
reevaluation~\cite{mora2012noisy}. Recent \emph{Tales of Tribute} work likewise
compares evolutionary training modes for a 20-weight bot
\cite{castejon2026tales}. These studies establish scripted-bot tuning and
mutation search; our distinction is family-disjoint held-out evaluation.

The closest conceptual precedent is adaptive selection by map type.
Fern{\'a}ndez-Ares et al. evolved specialized policies and selected among them
from online map characterization~\cite{fernandezAres2012map}. Our initial
map-conditioned method followed this broad idea and failed its development
gate. The final paper instead studies one generic policy, with exact-map
profiles, coordinates, orientation branches, and map signatures disabled. Its
distinctive evidence is family-disjoint and provenance-bound evaluation, not
map-conditioned policy selection.

\paragraph{Algorithm configuration.}
SMAC and irace formalize configuration across sets of problem instances and
separate tuning instances from unseen evaluation instances
\cite{hutter2011smac,lopezIbanez2016irace}. NTBEA is particularly relevant to
discrete game-agent configuration~\cite{lucas2018ntbea}, and direct game-agent
comparisons have found model-based configurators competitive while exposing
parameter interactions~\cite{lucas2019model}. Hyperband allocates increasing
resources to a shrinking candidate set~\cite{li2018hyperband}; our fixed
successive-halving schedule uses the same general resource-allocation idea.
Best-practice reviews warn that configurator comparisons are easily distorted
by scenario construction, over-tuning, and unequal budgets
\cite{eggensperger2019pitfalls}. We therefore do not claim optimizer
superiority: there is no SMAC, irace, or NTBEA baseline in this study. The
training algorithm is a fixed, inexpensive mechanism for producing one policy
whose evaluation can be audited.

\paragraph{Generalization and statistical evaluation.}
CoinRun and Procgen demonstrated that game agents can overfit even sizable
sets of levels and motivated explicit training--test distributions
\cite{cobbe2019coinrun,cobbe2020procgen}. The GVGAI learning track likewise
trains on visible levels and evaluates hidden ones; Balla et al. show that the
choice of training levels and environment stochasticity materially affect
generalization~\cite{balla2020generalisation}. Held-out level evaluation is
therefore established. Chrono Divide adds a different data problem: its maps
are not procedural independent draws, because exact copies and revisions can
coexist. We consequently split at a conservatively constructed family level
rather than at a filename level. The inferential unit is also the family, not
each correlated match.
Agarwal et al. showed how point estimates from a few tasks or runs can reverse
under uncertainty-aware analysis~\cite{agarwal2021precipice}. We use balanced
family estimands, family-clustered intervals, and diagnostic family bootstrap
and sign-flip analyses.

Protocol variation can change conclusions even within one standardized game
platform: Machado et al. audit divergent Arcade Learning Environment practices
and introduce controlled stochasticity~\cite{machado2018ale}, while Henderson
et al. document sensitivity to environment nondeterminism, random seeds, and
reporting choices in deep RL~\cite{henderson2018matters}. Our policies are
scripted rather than learned by RL, but the evaluation threat transfers to
stochastic head-to-head simulation. We therefore pin the runtime and expose
the simulator and participant random streams rather than treating repeat count
alone as reproducibility.

Shared seeds and reciprocal starts act as paired simulation controls. Common
random numbers are a classical variance-reduction design for comparing
policies under matched stochastic inputs~\cite{schruben2011crn}. In our
setting, an engine stream alone is insufficient because bot callbacks also
consume randomness. We therefore isolate participant-specific streams so an
extra draw by StrongBot cannot shift Supalosa's randomness. The resulting
contribution is an integrated evaluation protocol for a real scripted RTS
codebase: deterministic pairing, baseline isolation, family leakage control,
complete scheduler provenance, and honest negative gates in one executable
workflow.
