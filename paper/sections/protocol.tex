\section{Configuration and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Coordinate-free policy space}

The research interface exposes 28 StrongBot fields covering attack
composition, strategic plan, attack gates, defense radius, scouting, engineer
targets, static defense, all-in behavior, forced attacks, and emergency
defense. Each field has a finite declared choice set. The interface disables
default map profiles, exact-map tactics, coordinate routes, placement anchors,
orientation gates, and map signatures. A policy is one canonical JSON object
whose SHA-256 is its identity. Thus every search candidate receives game state
but no family ID or map-specific configuration. This shared coordinate-free
envelope defines the comparison. The fork's deployed default is not admissible
because its built-in profiles and exact-map tactics encode map identity; no
result estimates performance relative to that policy.

Each of five run indices deterministically creates 32 distinct policies.
Candidate 0 is the prospectively frozen generic reference (labeled
\texttt{default} in the immutable artifacts). Candidates 1--8 anchor each
alternative attack composition; candidates 9--17 anchor each alternative
strategic plan. Every non-reference candidate receives 6--14 additional
hash-ranked mutations over the declared fields. Generation reads no gameplay
outcome and fails on a duplicate policy hash.

\subsection{Five successive-halving searches}

Each run uses a hash-ranked nested schedule of 6, 12, and all 22 training
families, with start-count strata represented before rank filling. One new
engine-seed block is assigned per family and stage, and every policy--family
block has two reciprocal slots. Table~\ref{tab:search-budget} gives the exact
launched budget.

Successive-halving reduction uses game score plus a bounded terminal tie term.
For each of credits, units, buildings, combatants, harvesters, factories,
refineries, and construction yards, define

\begin{equation}
a_k=\frac{x_k-y_k}{|x_k|+|y_k|+c_k},\qquad
A=\frac{1}{8}\sum_k a_k,\qquad U=Y+0.04A,
\end{equation}

where $c_k=1000$ for credits and 1 otherwise. Since $A\in[-1,1]$, the tie term
cannot reverse the ordering loss $<$ draw $<$ win in an individual game.
Policies rank by equally family-weighted mean $U$, mean $Y$, worst-family mean
$U$, and policy hash. Stage 0 retains 12 policies; stage 1 retains 6; and stage
2 outputs six finalists per run. Evaluation claims use $Y$ only.

\subsection{Common-seed championship}

The five runs yield 30 unique finalists whose bytes are frozen. The
championship compares them on the training families under seeds that were not
used by the searches. Stage A evaluates all 30 policies on all 22 families
with one common seed block and reciprocal starts (1,320 games), retaining six.
Stage B evaluates those six with three additional common seed blocks per
family (792 games). The final ranking pools both stages and is lexicographic by
equally family-weighted score, mean of the five lowest family scores,
worst-family score, and policy hash. Terminal material cannot select the
champion. All policies within
a family--stage cell share the engine seed and opponent stream, so differences
are paired rather than confounded by run-specific randomness.

The selected policy differs from the reference in only seven stored fields
(Table~\ref{tab:policy}). The forced-attack combatant threshold is inactive
because forced attack is disabled, leaving six operational setting changes.

\subsection{Development adaptation and test freeze}

An earlier method selected among run finalists from three structural map
features. On the original 12-family development role, its
conditioned-minus-global estimate was $-0.025$; a training-only leave-one-family
audit also showed no positive response-surface gain. The method was retired
without opening test identities or outcomes. Method v2 removed conditioning and
used the common-seed championship to address a different training-only issue:
finalists from independent runs had not been compared under common randomness.

To avoid reusing the development evidence that motivated this change, method
v2 used an outcome-free pool of the four original reserves and seven review
families. A technical adjudicator admitted reviews only if both reciprocal
passive probes reached tick 250, starts were valid, and warnings belonged to a
predeclared nonfatal set. A hash rank fixed ten primaries and one substitute.
The three-phase gate comprised 32 repeated fresh-process games, 88 full-pool
compatibility games, and 320 outcome-bearing signal games. Its single
unblinding required positive one-sided 80\% lower bounds for both
champion-minus-reference and champion-minus-0.5. Both passed; this was a
permissive futility screen, not confirmatory evidence.

Only then were the champion, reference, source/runtime commitments, test analysis,
and attempt budget frozen. The confirmatory schedule crossed all 16 test
families, eight new seed blocks, two methods, and two reciprocal slots, for
\ConfirmatoryGameCount{} games in 128 indivisible shards.

\subsection{Estimands and uncertainty}

For family $f$, seed block $b$, and slot $s$, let

\begin{equation}
d_{fbs}=Y_{\mathrm{champion},fbs}-Y_{\mathrm{reference},fbs},
\qquad D_{fb}=\frac{d_{fb0}+d_{fb1}}{2}.
\end{equation}

The primary estimand is the equal-family mean of the 128 balanced $D_{fb}$
values. Let $e_{fb}=D_{fb}-\widehat\Delta$, $G=16$, and $N=128$. The frozen
family-cluster variance is

\begin{equation}
\widehat V(\widehat\Delta)=
\frac{G}{G-1}\frac{\sum_f\left(\sum_b e_{fb}\right)^2}{N^2}.
\label{eq:cluster}
\end{equation}

The primary relative gate requires the two-sided 95\% Student-$t_{15}$ interval
to lie above zero. A distinct absolute gate applies the same estimator to
reciprocal champion scores and requires the one-sided 95\% lower bound for
champion score minus 0.5 to exceed zero. Both gates and all results were
specified before test opening; the frozen protocol requires both gates for
joint success.
