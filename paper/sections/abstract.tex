\begin{abstract}
Scripted game agents are often tuned on a small set of maps, so reported gains
can conflate policy quality with map memorization, start position, simulator
randomness, or a contaminated opponent implementation. We present a
reproducible evaluation and configuration pipeline for StrongBot in Chrono
Divide, a browser reconstruction of \emph{Command \& Conquer: Red Alert 2}.
The pipeline groups map revisions into families, screens simulator
compatibility without policy outcomes, isolates a pinned Supalosa opponent,
controls engine and participant randomness, swaps physical starts, seals
held-out outcomes, and binds every result to source, runtime, map, plan, and
scheduler commitments. Five 32-policy successive-halving searches followed by
a common-seed championship select one coordinate-free generic policy using
training families only. In a frozen 512-game evaluation over 16 sealed
families, the selected policy scores \ChampionScore{} versus \DefaultScore{}
for the shipped StrongBot default. The equally family-weighted improvement is
\ImprovementEstimate{} (family-clustered 95\% CI
[\ImprovementCILower{},~\ImprovementCIUpper{}]); 14 family effects are positive
and two are zero. A separate prespecified gate does not establish absolute
superiority: the one-sided 95\% lower bound for the selected policy's score
margin above 0.5 is \ChampionAbsoluteLower{}. Paired outcomes show that the
configured policy primarily replaces losses with survival or wins. Component
and terminal-state analyses are consistent with an infantry-rush policy that
converts more resources into fielded units, but neither endpoints nor the
multiplicity-controlled contrasts establish that mechanism. We delimit the
result to one opponent, matchup, and supported map population and release the
author-owned reproducibility layer.

\keywords{real-time strategy games \and evolutionary computation \and
algorithm configuration \and reproducible evaluation \and distribution shift}
\end{abstract}
