\section{Conclusion}

We reconstructed an exploratory Chrono Divide bot project into a deterministic,
family-disjoint, provenance-bound evaluation of scripted policy configuration.
Five successive-halving searches and a common-seed championship selected one
generic StrongBot policy using training families only. On 16 sealed test
families, that policy improves score over the shipped default by
\ImprovementEstimate{} (family-clustered 95\% CI
[\ImprovementCILower{},~\ImprovementCIUpper{}]), with positive effects on 14
families and zero effects on two. The practical gain is mainly fewer losses:
many default losses become tick-cap draws, and fewer become wins.

The study also gives a necessary negative answer. The champion's one-sided
lower score margin against Supalosa is \ChampionAbsoluteLower{}, so reliable
absolute superiority is not established. Post-confirmatory evidence points to
an infantry-rush configuration and greater conversion of resources into
fielded units, but neither endpoint snapshots nor any component contrast
establishes a mechanism after familywise multiplicity control. The contribution
is therefore a reproducible configuration-and-evaluation workflow plus a strong
bounded relative result---not a new game environment, a new general optimizer,
or a claim of broad game-AI dominance.
