\subsection{Relative improvement passes}

The reference scores \DefaultScore{} (\DefaultWins{} win, \DefaultDraws{} draws,
\DefaultLosses{} losses), whereas the champion scores \ChampionScore{}
(\ChampionWins{} wins, \ChampionDraws{} draws, \ChampionLosses{} losses). The
equally-family-weighted champion-minus-reference estimate is
\ImprovementEstimate{}, with clustered standard error \ImprovementSE{} and
95\% interval [\ImprovementCILower{},~\ImprovementCIUpper{}]. Its lower endpoint
is above zero, passing the relative gate.

Figure~\ref{fig:family-effects} shows every family effect.
\PositiveFamilyCount{} are positive, \ZeroFamilyCount{} are exactly zero, and
none is negative at the family-average level. Effects range from
\MinimumFamilyEffect{} to \MaximumFamilyEffect{}; thus the mean is not produced by a single
family, although effect magnitude is heterogeneous. These points are estimates
from 16 games per method and family, not independent confidence intervals.

As post-confirmatory sensitivity checks, an independent Python reducer reread
all \ConfirmatoryGameCount{} raw completion records and reproduced the point estimates, standard
errors, and bounds exactly. A 100,000-replicate family bootstrap yielded a 95\%
interval [\ImprovementBootstrapLower{},~\ImprovementBootstrapUpper{}], and the
exact family sign-flip two-sided value was
$p=\ImprovementSignFlipP{}$. These checks support robustness of the relative
result but did not replace the frozen estimator.

\subsection{Absolute superiority does not pass}

The champion's score margin over 0.5 is \ChampionAbsoluteMargin{}, with
family-clustered standard error \ChampionAbsoluteSE{}. Its prespecified
one-sided 95\% lower bound is \ChampionAbsoluteLower{}, so the absolute-strength
gate fails. Because the frozen protocol requires both gates, its joint success
criterion also fails. \FamiliesAboveHalfCount{} family scores are above 0.5,
\FamiliesEqualHalfCount{} equal 0.5, and \FamiliesBelowHalfCount{} fall below
0.5. The diagnostic family-bootstrap interval for champion score,
[\ChampionBootstrapLower{}, \ChampionBootstrapUpper{}], leads
to the same caution. Therefore, the correct positive result is not ``StrongBot reliably
beats Supalosa.'' It is ``the configured StrongBot robustly improves the frozen
generic reference against pinned Supalosa on this family population.''

\subsection{The gain is mostly avoided losses}

Because both methods use the same family, engine seed, opponent stream, and
physical slot, the \ConfirmatoryGamesPerMethod{} per-method games form direct
outcome pairs. Figure~\ref{fig:transitions} shows their transition matrix. Of
\DefaultLosses{} reference losses, \LossToDrawPairCount{} become champion draws
and \LossToWinPairCount{} become champion wins. \DrawToWinPairCount{} reference
draws become wins, while \DrawToLossPairCount{} regress to losses. Overall, \ImprovedPairCount{}
pairs improve, \RegressedPairCount{} regress, and \UnchangedPairCount{} have
the same frozen score. The policy gains \ChampionWinGainCount{} wins and avoids
\ChampionLossAvoidedCount{} losses relative
to the reference, but its most common improvement is loss-to-tick-cap survival.
