\subsection{RQ2: scoped mechanisms solve distinct failures}

The mechanism studies in Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms} are paired replications on
fresh cases. Allied west rush+guard changes the control from
\AlliedControlWdl{} to \AlliedWinnerWdl{}, with paired-score lower bound
\AlliedPairedLower{}. The Soviet counterpart changes
\SovietControlWdl{} to \SovietWinnerWdl{} (lower
\textit{L}=\SovietPairedLower{}). The bottom progress-gated retarget changes
\BottomControlWdl{} to \BottomWinnerWdl{} (lower
\textit{L}=\BottomPairedLower{}) without increasing losses.

The effect is not merely a broad profile change. In separate outcome-free
gates, each mechanism changes normalized action traces in every intended-active
cell and is exactly equal to control in \AlliedInactiveCells{},
\SovietInactiveCells{}, and \BottomInactiveCells{} intended-inactive cells,
respectively. These results support three local causal statements about the
implemented policy: the west profiles create the large west improvements; the
bottom retarget converts otherwise tied cases into wins; and the interventions
remain isolated outside their declared conditions. They do not imply that any
one component explains all 720 confirmation games.

\subsection{RQ3: reciprocal macro scope repairs Peak}

The six-arm Peak screen isolated macro-strategy scope from tactical/bot scope.
The $2\times2$ factorial estimated a +0.243 macro-scope main effect, a -0.007
tactical-scope main effect, and a -0.014 interaction. The frozen ranking
therefore selected \texttt{strategy\_both}: apply the existing macro profile at
both reciprocal starts and leave tactical scope unchanged.

On 180 sealed cases, the deployed control records
\PeakControlWins{}/\PeakControlDraws{}/\PeakControlLosses{}, whereas the
unchanged reciprocal policy records
\PeakWins{}/\PeakDraws{}/\PeakLosses{}. The win rate rises from
\PeakControlWinRate{} to \PeakWinRate{}; the new policy's Wilson lower bound is
\PeakWilsonLower{}. Across the matched cases, \PeakImproved{} improve,
\PeakTied{} tie, and \PeakWorsened{} worsen. The mean paired-score gain is
\PeakPairedMean{}, with one-sided lower bound \PeakPairedLower{}. An
equal-weight country--start analysis gives lower bound
\PeakClusterLower{}. All nine countries, both starts, both participant sides,
and both slots have more wins than losses under the confirmed policy.

Against Advanced, StrongBot records

pinned Supalosa records
\AdvancedSupalosaWins{}/\AdvancedSupalosaDraws{}/\AdvancedSupalosaLosses{}
(Table~\ref{tab:advanced}). The paired-score difference is

Successive prospectively frozen overlay, order-only, and early-production

We therefore retain the negative result: StrongBot is reliably stronger than
Supalosa on the two tested Supalosa matchups, but it is not a generally
dominant Chrono Divide bot.
