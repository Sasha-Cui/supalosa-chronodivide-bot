Full-game real-time strategy (RTS) agents are easy to overstate:
apparent strength can come from favorable starts, map-specific tuning, coupled
randomness, modified opponents, or proxy wins. We use the existing Chrono
Divide browser reconstruction of \emph{Red Alert 2} as a deterministic headless
testbed and develop StrongBot, a scripted derivative of the public Supalosa
bot. StrongBot combines start-scoped rush and home-guard profiles,
progress-gated building retargeting, and literal all-building elimination.
Primary evaluations pin the external opponent, isolate participant randomness,
balance nine countries, physical starts, and participant slots, suppress
resignation symmetrically, and bind outcomes to source, runtime, seeds, and
scheduler records. On 720 fresh Heck Freezes Over games, StrongBot records
\HfoWins{}/\HfoDraws{}/\HfoLosses{} (win rate \HfoWinRate{}; one-sided 95\%
Wilson lower bound \HfoWilsonLower{}; country-by-start lower bound
\HfoClusterLower{}). On Peak of Perfection, a factorial screen isolates
reciprocal macro-profile scope; the unchanged winner records
\PeakWins{}/\PeakDraws{}/\PeakLosses{} on 180 fresh cases versus
\PeakControlWins{}/\PeakControlDraws{}/\PeakControlLosses{} for the deployed
control, with paired lower improvement \PeakPairedLower{}. Against an
independently sourced RA2Web Advanced bot, StrongBot falls to
\AdvancedStrongWins{}/\AdvancedStrongDraws{}/\AdvancedStrongLosses{}. The
bounded contribution is reliable superiority over pinned Supalosa on two maps,
replicated scoped mechanisms, and an explicit cross-opponent limit.
