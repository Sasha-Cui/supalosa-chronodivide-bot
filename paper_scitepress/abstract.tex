Scripted game-agent gains can reflect map memorization, favorable starts,
uncontrolled randomness, or contaminated baselines. We present a
leakage-resistant evaluation protocol and reproducible configuration pipeline
for StrongBot in Chrono Divide, an existing browser reconstruction of
\emph{Command \& Conquer: Red Alert 2}.
The workflow groups map revisions into families, screens compatibility without
policy outcomes, isolates a pinned external Supalosa opponent, controls engine
and participant randomness, swaps physical starts, seals test outcomes, and
records source, runtime, map, and scheduler provenance. Five
\OptimizerCandidateCount{}-policy deterministic searches with successive
halving use training families; a common-seed championship selects one
coordinate-free policy. In \ConfirmatoryGameCount{} frozen games over
\TestFamilyCount{} sealed families, the champion scores \ChampionScore{} versus
\DefaultScore{} for a prospectively frozen generic StrongBot reference. The
equally family-weighted improvement is \ImprovementEstimate{}
(family-clustered 95\% CI
[\ImprovementCILower{},~\ImprovementCIUpper{}]);
\PositiveFamilyCount{} family effects are positive and \ZeroFamilyCount{} are
zero. The prespecified joint two-gate objective fails: the absolute-strength
gate's one-sided 95\%
lower bound for the champion's margin above 0.5 is
\ChampionAbsoluteLower{}. Improvement primarily replaces losses with survival
or wins. The study therefore establishes configuration improvement within a
coordinate-free policy class, not superiority over Supalosa, the deployed
StrongBot default, other opponents, or other matchups. An identity-neutral
aggregate artifact regenerates every table and figure.
